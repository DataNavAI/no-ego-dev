# SEO-first community and interest destination MVP pattern

Use this pattern when a consumer community, hobby, interest, or entity product aims to become a search destination rather than relying mainly on an app store or social feed. Fandom is one applicable example, not the default domain.

## Research pattern

1. Map query families by the user's underlying job, not by isolated keywords. Useful classes include entity facts, recognition or learning, freshness and deadlines, transactions and trust, comparisons, and local discovery.
2. Use live autocomplete as directional demand evidence when volume tools are unavailable. Record the date, locale assumptions, exact seed queries, and that no volume is claimed.
3. Inspect the current result type for each cluster. A query with apparent demand and strong incumbents is not automatically a viable wedge.
4. Separate informational SEO opportunities from software workflows. Prefer an interactive product only when interaction materially improves comprehension, confidence, action, or repeat use.
5. Look for an adjacent share behavior that is already natural to the community: identity signaling, competence, belonging, utility, or timely participation.

## Product synthesis pattern

A strong SEO-first consumer wedge combines:

> query answer → guided learning/action → proof or useful share artifact

The landing page must deliver the promised answer immediately. The guided action and optional share step follow value; neither may block, obscure, or replace the answer.

Across interests such as sports, games, entertainment, crafts, collecting, local activities, or other enthusiast communities, a useful shape is:

- a concise, source-backed, visibly fresh answer;
- visual structure that makes confusing entities, choices, or timelines easy to understand;
- a short challenge, checklist, comparison, planner, or personalized artifact that advances the same job; and
- a share result that expresses identity, competence, or utility and links to the canonical experience.

The answer, guided action, and share loop must solve the same user problem. Do not bolt an unrelated game, referral mechanic, or social gate onto an informational page.

## SEO scope guardrails

- Prefer one substantial canonical page that satisfies a coherent query cluster over thin pages that merely rearrange the same facts.
- Start with a small, manually verified set before scaling programmatically.
- Treat freshness, provenance, correction handling, image/media rights, and structured internal links as product requirements.
- Avoid doorway pages and mass-generated filler.
- For visual recognition involving people, children, sensitive traits, or licensed media, avoid biometric or rights-sensitive features unless legal, privacy, safety, and media-rights requirements are explicitly resolved. A curated learning or comparison flow is usually a safer wedge.

## Validation

Test behavior rather than stated enthusiasm:

- Can users complete a realistic query task without returning to search?
- How quickly do they reach the answer and confidence moment?
- Do they understand freshness, status, and provenance distinctions?
- Do they complete the guided action because it helps, rather than because the answer was withheld?
- Do they actually download, copy, send, or post the share artifact?
- Do referred users enter the same value journey rather than a generic homepage?

Set numeric go/no-go thresholds before observing results. Pair SEO probes with task-based usability tests; rankings alone do not prove satisfaction, and positive interviews do not prove search demand.
